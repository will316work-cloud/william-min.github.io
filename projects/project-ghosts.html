Ghosts in the Toy Box

Survival Horror, Roguelike, 2D Top-Down Game Designer, Level Designer, Lead Game Engineer

"You are trapped in a sealed haunted toy box as a tiny toy in a 2D top-down survival horror roguelike. Every toy before you who tried to escape returned as a possessed plaything corpse. You only have a toy gun and a tablet that reveals the monsters' visions. Will that be enough to truly escape?"

↑ Top

Features

  • Find and vanquish invisible spirits by seeing through their visions with a frog tablet.
  • Fire a pop gun, deploy night lights, and throw marbles collected from destroyed spirits.
  • Evade a statue enemy and dart traps that grow stronger with each destroyed spirit.
  • Escape a claustrophobic, randomly generated toy box by killing all the spirits and reaching the exit.
↑ Top

Project Goals

  • Create an objective that necessitates heavy use of the vision-revealing frog tablet.
  • Design challenging enemies that test the player's use of the tablet and navigation in a dark, cramped maze.
  • Demonstrate my familiarity with and passion for designing enemies as core drivers of gameplay identity.

Constraints

  • Time: 15 weeks of development

The Process

  1. Created prototypes and drew insight from playtests.
  2. Built base abilities, interactables, and environment systems for the core gameplay loop.
  3. Built one enemy that requires tablet engagement and another that requires environmental awareness.
  4. Polished game elements with visual effects, lighting, sound effects, music, and audio managers.

An Objective That Requires the Tablet

Made invisible spirits — which need to be destroyed — visible on the map only through the tablet.

Thought Process

  • A big challenge was incentivizing players to use the novel mechanic of seeing through enemy visions.
  • An enemy invisible to the naked eye but visible through the tablet would push players to rely on it as their main survival tool.
  • The invisible spirit aesthetic reinforced the haunted toy box theme.

Results

  • Invisible spirits made engaging with the frog tablet fun and meaningful.
  • Some playtesters found the tablet confusing at first, but quickly recognized the objective.

Cramped, Randomized Level Design

The toy box generates many arrangements of colored, lego-like brick tiles across a 5x5 area.

Thought Process

  • Designed the level generator to mix open and cramped tiles, creating potential hiding spots for the invisible spirits.
  • Skewed generation toward more enclosed tiles than open ones for claustrophobic navigation through the small maze.

Results

  • The cramped tiles created many hiding places for spirits and player alike.
  • The randomized tiles turned finding spirits into a test of visual memory.
  • The claustrophobic layout made it easy for enemies and other dangers to sneak up on the player.

Environmental Enemy and Seeker Enemy

Created a cymbal monkey toy that switches bodies with, and blends into, a set of decoy clones, plus a swarm of clown dart toys that periodically fire darts.

Thought Process

  • Enemies act as dangers in the maze — some roam to search and chase the player, others serve as obstacles that slow down or box players in.
    • The cymbal monkey chases the player when unobserved while blending in with its scattered clones.
    • The clown dart shooters spawn in different locations and directions, threatening multiple sightlines at once.
    • Together, the dart shooters can box in the player while the cymbal monkey closes in as they're occupied dodging both.
  • Enemies either require checking the frog tablet or make the environment more dangerous, splitting the player's attention between the tablet and navigation.
    • Without getting hurt, the cymbal monkey's position can only be confirmed by checking its vision on the frog tablet.
    • The clown dart shooters make certain sightlines dangerous to linger on and check the tablet, especially while evading the cymbal monkey.
  • To keep these interactions intimidating but not punishing, implemented a health bar and invincibility frames — an unexpected hit or a surrounded position doesn't end the run outright, but signals the player to retreat, regroup, and avoid repeating the mistake.
    • The player has 10 health.
    • The cymbal monkey deals 3 damage on contact.
    • Clown darts deal 1 damage.

Results

  • Playtesters were startled repeatedly without feeling too frustrated when they died after failing to dodge danger too many times.
  • Playtesters stayed focused and vigilant throughout.
  • Playtesters tried killing the enemies, but to no avail.

Upgrades for Player and Enemies

Destroying a spirit grants the player items and either spawns a new enemy or enhances an existing enemy's abilities or behavior.

Thought Process

  • Upgrades relieve tension by restoring item counts from a limited pool.
  • New enemy spawns and upgrades raise tension through more threatening abilities and new tricks:
    • Clown dart shooters can fire a volley of flaming darts, which light up nearby areas and deal 2 damage each.
    • The cymbal monkey's clones patrol the maze and freeze when looked at without damaging the player, further masking the real toy's approach and teleportation.

Results

  • Playtesters didn't notice rising tension until enemy changes became visually obvious, like the fiery darts, or the cymbal monkey grew generally more intense.
  • Player upgrades were a nice tension release, though their impact was often middling.

Key Takeaways

  • Introducing players to novel gameplay and mechanics is harder than making it engaging once they're using it.
  • Player gameplay defines the interaction with the game, but level and enemy design and composition are what shape the experience.
  • Frequent iteration and playtesting were the foundation of this project's refinement, quickly surfacing what was worth preserving, revising, or cutting.
